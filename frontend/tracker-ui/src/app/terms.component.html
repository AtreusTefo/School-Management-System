1. Who may use this system

Assignment Tracker is provided by [School name] ("the school") for the use of its enrolled students, its teaching staff, and the administrators it appoints. Accounts are created by a school administrator. There is no public registration, and accounts may not be created, transferred or shared by anyone else.

By signing in, you agree to these terms.

2. Your account

  • You are issued a sign-in ID by the school. It identifies you in every record you create, including the audit log, and it cannot be changed.
  • An account created for you begins with a temporary password chosen by somebody else. You must set your own before the account can be used for anything else.
  • You are responsible for keeping your password confidential and for activity carried out under your account. Do not share your credentials or let another person use your session.
  • Changing your password requires your current one, even while you are already signed in. An administrator can reset a password, but cannot read an existing one.
  • If you forget your password you can reset it yourself, provided the school holds a contact email address for you. A link is sent to that address; it works once and expires after {{ resetLinkMinutes }} minutes. Requesting a new link cancels any earlier one.
  • The forgotten-password page gives the same response whether or not what you typed matched an account, so that it cannot be used to find out who holds one. If no link arrives, check the address the school has on file rather than assuming the request failed.
  • A reset link is as good as your password until it is used. Do not forward one, and tell the school if you receive a link you did not ask for.
  • Tell the school immediately if you believe someone else has used your account.

3. Submitting work

  • Work is handed in as a PDF document only, up to {{ maxUploadMb }} megabytes. Files are checked by their actual content, so renaming another kind of file will not make it acceptable.
  • Uploading a new file replaces the previous one for that submission. Only the most recent file is kept, and it is the one that will be marked.
  • Once you have handed work in, you cannot withdraw it yourself. A teacher can reopen a submission if there is a genuine reason to.
  • A due date is recorded against each assignment, and work handed in after it is visible as such. How lateness affects a mark is the school's academic decision, not the software's.

4. Attendance

  • Attendance is recorded per lesson, not once a day. A register marks each student present, late, absent or excused, and may carry a short note.
  • Only the teacher who teaches that lesson, or an administrator, can take or change its register. Students cannot record or amend their own attendance, and cannot see anyone else's.
  • Taking a register again corrects it. There is one record per student per lesson per date, so the latest entry is the record - there is no separate history of earlier versions to appeal against.
  • A register can only be taken against a date the lesson genuinely falls on - the right weekday, inside the right term, for a student actually enrolled in that class. This is enforced, not merely expected.
  • What attendance means for a student - whether an absence is authorised, and what follows from a pattern of them - is the school's decision under its own policies. The system records what happened; it does not judge it. If you believe a mark on your record is wrong, raise it with the teacher who took that register.

5. Messages and notifications

  • Messaging in this system is for school business. It is not private mail: messages are stored in full on the school's system, and the school can access them where it has a proper reason to.
  • Who you can write to is decided by the system, not by you. Students can message the teachers who teach them and any administrator. Teachers can message their own students, other teachers and administrators. Students cannot message other students, and attempting to work around that restriction is a breach of section 7 below.
  • A message cannot be edited or withdrawn once sent, and the recipient can see when you first opened theirs. Write accordingly.
  • Bullying, harassment, or anything you would not say in a classroom is a matter for the school's disciplinary process, and the stored message is evidence in it.
  • Notifications tell you that a mark has been posted, that a register marked you absent, or that work is nearly due. They are a convenience, not a formal notice: they arrive only inside this app, nothing is emailed or texted to you or to anyone at home, and you remain responsible for your own due dates whether or not a notification reached you.
  • You cannot delete a notification. Marking it read is the only change you can make, because it is a record of what you were told and when.

6. Academic honesty

Work you submit must be your own, produced in line with the school's academic integrity policy. Submitting someone else's work, or allowing another student to submit yours, is a matter for the school's disciplinary process. Because every submission and mark is recorded against the account that made it, the system's records may be used as evidence in such a process.

7. Acceptable use

You must not:

  • Attempt to access another person's account, submissions, files, marks, attendance record, messages or notifications.
  • Attempt to bypass, probe or interfere with the system's access controls, or with the checks that protect the integrity of its records.
  • Upload anything unlawful, malicious, or intended to disrupt the system or the people using it.
  • Use automated means to extract data beyond what the interface offers you.

The school may suspend or remove access for a breach of these rules.

8. Marks and records

Marks recorded in this system are entered by teaching staff. Totals, percentages and performance levels shown to you are calculated from those marks each time they are displayed, so a correction to a mark is reflected everywhere immediately.

The system is a record of submission and assessment. It does not itself determine progression, awards, or any other academic outcome - those remain the school's decisions, made under its own policies. If you believe a mark is wrong, raise it with the teacher concerned.

9. Availability

The school aims to keep the system available but does not guarantee uninterrupted access. It may be unavailable during maintenance, or because of failures outside the school's control. Do not leave a submission until the last moment on the assumption that the system will be reachable.
