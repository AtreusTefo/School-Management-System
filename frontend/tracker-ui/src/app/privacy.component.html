1. Who this applies to

This policy covers everyone with an account on Assignment Tracker as operated by [School name] ("the school", "we"): students, teachers, and administrators. The school is the data controller and decides how the information described here is used.

2. What we collect, and why

The system holds only what it needs to identify a person, decide what they may do, and record the work they hand in or mark.

Account and identity

  • Sign-in ID and role. Every account is issued an ID (for example a student ID beginning STU) and holds one role - teacher, student, or administrator. This is what determines what the account is allowed to see and do.
  • Password. Stored only as a one-way BCrypt hash. The password itself is never stored, never written to a log, and never returned by the system - including to administrators, who can reset a password but cannot read one.

Personal details

  • Name - first, middle where given, and last.
  • National identity number - recorded so that one person entered twice is recognised as the same person rather than becoming two accounts.
  • Sex, derived and never stored. The national identity number encodes this in one of its digits, and the system reads it from there whenever it is displayed. There is no separate field holding it, nothing asks you for it, and it cannot be set or corrected on its own - correcting the identity number is what corrects it.
  • Date of birth and date enrolled.
  • Contact email address and phone number, where provided. Neither is a sign-in credential - your sign-in ID is. The email address has one further use: if you ask to reset a forgotten password, the reset link is sent there, and you may identify yourself by that address instead of your ID when asking.

Administrator accounts do not have a personal detail record at all - only an ID, a role and a credential.

Academic records

  • Enrolment - which class a student belongs to, and which teacher teaches which subject to that class.
  • Timetable - the terms of the school year, the periods a day is divided into, and which lesson is taught in which period on which weekday. This describes the school rather than any one person, but it is what makes the attendance record below meaningful.
  • Attendance - for each lesson on each date, whether a student was present, late, absent or excused, an optional note, and which member of staff recorded it. Attendance is held per lesson rather than per day, so the record shows which specific lessons were missed and not merely that a day was incomplete.
  • Assignments and submissions - the work set, its due date, and each student's submission state and the time it changed.
  • Uploaded files - the PDF a student hands in, along with its original filename, size, and a checksum used to detect corruption.
  • Marks - the score awarded out of a maximum, who recorded it, and when.

Attendance rates and percentages are never stored. They are worked out from the individual records each time they are shown, so a corrected register corrects every figure built on it.

Messages and notifications

  • Messages you send. The text, who wrote it, who it was sent to, when it was sent, and when the recipient first opened it. Messages are stored in full and are not encrypted end-to-end: they are school correspondence held on the school's system, not private mail.
  • Notifications. A short title and description of something that happened to your records - a mark posted, an absence recorded, work falling due - together with when it was raised and when you read it. The wording is written once, at the moment it is raised, and is never rewritten afterwards, so it remains an accurate record of what you were told and when.

Who may message whom is restricted and enforced on the server. A student can write only to the teachers who actually teach them and to an administrator - not to other students. Staff cannot read a conversation they are not a party to.

Password resets

  • When you ask to reset a forgotten password, the system stores a one-way hash of the reset token - never the token itself - together with the account it belongs to and the time it expires. The link stops working 30 minutes after it is issued, or as soon as it has been used once, and using one link invalidates any others outstanding for that account.
  • A record that a reset was requested is written to the audit log described below. The link itself is never written there or to any log.

Activity record

  • Audit log. Every creation, change and deletion made through the system is recorded, together with a description of what changed and the username and role of the person who made it, captured at that moment.

3. Who can see what

Access is decided by role and enforced on the server, not merely hidden in the interface.

  • Students see their own timetable, their own submissions, their own uploaded files, their own marks and their own attendance. A student cannot see another student's work, marks, attendance, or class register - asking for someone else's is refused outright rather than quietly answered with their own.
  • Teachers see their own teaching week, the students in the classes they teach, the submissions for the work they set, and the marks they and other teachers of that course have recorded. A register may be taken and read only by the teacher who teaches that particular lesson, or by an administrator.
  • Nobody can read another person's notifications, and no part of this system offers a way to. A conversation is visible only to its two participants - an administrator is not given a view of other people's messages.
  • Administrators can see account records and the audit log, maintain the subjects, classes, courses, terms and periods, and see which subjects and students any teacher teaches and which teachers teach any student.

One deliberate exception is worth stating: a student's attendance history may be read by any member of staff, not only the teachers who teach them, because attendance is a pastoral matter that often needs to be looked at by someone outside the classroom. Every other record listed above is scoped to the people directly involved in it.

4. How the information is protected

  • Passwords are stored only as one-way hashes.
  • Access requires an authenticated session, and every request that changes data must carry a token that prevents another website from making that request on your behalf.
  • Uploaded files are stored inside the database as part of the same transaction as the submission itself, so a file can never be left without the record it belongs to, or the reverse.
  • Uploads are accepted only as PDF documents, and are checked by content rather than by trusting the name they arrive with.
  • Error messages returned to the browser never include database or internal system detail.
  • The forgotten-password page answers identically whether the ID or email you typed matched an account or not. This is deliberate: anyone can reach that page without signing in, so a different answer for a real account would let a stranger use it to discover who has one. It also means the page cannot confirm to you that your own address was recognised.
  • Reset links are stored only as one-way hashes, expire after 30 minutes and work once. Requests are rate-limited, so asking repeatedly does not produce a stream of valid links.

5. How long it is kept

Academic records - submissions, uploaded files, marks and attendance - are retained for as long as the school requires them, in line with its own records retention policy: [state the retention period].

Five behaviours are worth stating plainly, because they are deliberate:

  • An account cannot be deleted while work depends on it. A student with a recorded mark or a submission on file cannot be removed, because doing so would destroy the record of work that was genuinely done. Removing it must be a separate, deliberate act.
  • A lesson cannot be removed from the timetable once a register has been taken against it. A register records who was actually in a room on a date, and rearranging the timetable afterwards is not a reason for that to stop being true.
  • Taking a register again corrects it; it does not add a second one. There is only ever one attendance record per student per lesson per date, so the current record is the current answer and there is no history of superseded versions to accumulate.
  • Messages and notifications are removed with the account. Unlike marks, submissions and attendance - which are records of something that happened, and which prevent an account being deleted at all - correspondence and alerts describe a live relationship. When an account is removed, its notifications and its conversations are deleted with it, including the other party's copy of those conversations. The audit log is what carries the administrative trail past that point.
  • The audit log is append-only. Entries are never edited or deleted, and they record the username and role as they were at the time. An entry therefore remains readable after the account it names has been removed.

6. Sharing

Information in this system is not sold, and is not shared with advertisers or any third party for their own purposes. It is available to school staff in the roles described above, and may be disclosed where the school is legally required to do so.

7. Cookies

The system sets a session cookie so that you stay signed in as you move between pages, and a token used to protect against cross-site request forgery. There are no advertising or analytics cookies, and no third-party tracking.

8. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask for a copy of the personal information held about you, to have inaccurate information corrected, or to object to how it is used. Corrections to a name, a national identity number, a date of birth or contact details can usually be made directly by a school administrator; a wrong attendance mark is corrected by the teacher who takes that lesson's register, and a wrong mark by the teacher who recorded it.
